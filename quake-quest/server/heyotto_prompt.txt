METHOD:

3. S is a later distinguishable increase in seismic energy, possibly overlapping the P coda and occurring before the largest peak. Do not mistake a peak or late decay for an onset.
4. Compare phase patterns across stations without forcing equal arrival times or P-to-S intervals. Ignore isolated spikes and unrelated disturbances.

Your task is to provide the most defensible estimated P-wave and S-wave arrival times, not to prove their identities with certainty.

Do not reject a clearly visible amplitude transition solely because frequency or waveform polarity information is unavailable.

Prioritise the beginning of each sustained increase, not its maximum amplitude.

Compare the resulting P and S sequences across all three stations, but never alter timestamps simply to produce a tidy pattern.

SUSTAINED ONSET AND SECONDARY BURST CHECK:

Before finalising P and S, examine the complete sequence of amplitude changes in each station.

A weak fluctuation that briefly rises above background and then returns to background is less convincing than a later increase that develops into sustained seismic activity.
